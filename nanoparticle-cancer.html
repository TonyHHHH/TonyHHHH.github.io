Nanoparticle-Based Cancer Cell Detection

Aim / Goal

Investigate whether targeted nanoparticles could distinguish cancer cells from normal cells using fluorescence-based detection.

What we did

We compared LNCaP cancer cells with PNT2 normal cells using fluorescence microscopy. Blue DAPI staining visualised nuclei, while the green channel represented nanoparticle signal. Fluorescence was quantified using corrected total cell fluorescence to compare binding between the two cell populations.

Results

LNCaP cells showed a much stronger green nanoparticle signal than PNT2 cells. Mean CTCF was approximately 159,716 a.u. for LNCaP compared with 62,306 a.u. for PNT2, corresponding to roughly 2.6× higher fluorescence in the cancer-cell population.

Experimental limitations included unbound nanoparticles, floating cells and variation in washing and nanoparticle preparation, which informed recommendations for improved sample handling.
