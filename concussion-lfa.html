Lateral Flow Assay for Sideline Concussion Screening

Aim / Goal

Develop a rapid, non-invasive salivary lateral flow assay concept for sideline concussion screening using S100B as the target biomarker. The concept used silver nanoparticle enhancement and was intended to support, rather than replace, existing clinical concussion assessment.

What we did

The project combined device engineering and assay development. In the laboratory we practised the LFA workflow using PSA as a model analyte, including pad preparation, antibody striping, strip assembly, sample loading and assay testing. In parallel, I designed an LFA cassette in SolidWorks and contributed to the final group design, which was 3D printed and evaluated for strip alignment, assembly and usability.

Quantitative analysis

Fiji ImageJ was used to quantify test-line intensity, while MATLAB generated intensity profiles, detected test/control peaks and calculated the T/C ratio across concentration levels. The ImageJ calibration produced an R² of 0.9179, and comparison between MATLAB and ImageJ outputs produced an R² of 0.9863.

Results & learning

Initial PSA strip testing exposed practical issues including background staining, membrane orientation and inconsistent line formation. These results were used to define improvements for future assay assembly, while the quantitative image-analysis workflows showed a clear path toward more objective LFA interpretation.
